# Start here: let the agent carry the organizing

You do not need a finished résumé, a clear job title or neatly organized career notes to begin. This kit helps you and your preferred AI assistant build those together. You bring your experience and decisions; the agent does the first pass at reading, organizing, drafting and keeping track.

Use any agent you are comfortable with. You do not need to run code or understand the technical files to start. The optional local CLI helps manage files later; it is not required for this conversation.

## First, see what is possible here

Your agent should briefly explain the experience: make sense of your career material, build truthful résumé language, explore roles, and bring you a small set of decisions. Then it should say what this particular session can do now, what it can help you do manually, what would need optional setup, and what it cannot yet verify. That answer should come from available tools and observed results, not the agent's brand name.

You can begin with the capabilities already available. You do not need to connect another service or set up automation first. If a small optional improvement would help your immediate goal, the agent can explain it and its tradeoff. If there is no scheduler, for example, it can save a check-in for when you return; it should not pretend a reminder will arrive.

## What to bring

Bring whatever is easy to find and appropriate to share: old résumés, career notes, project descriptions, a portfolio, permitted feedback, a job posting, or a messy account of things you remember doing. Older material can reveal strengths that disappeared from a newer résumé. Incomplete and contradictory materials are normal. Nothing on that list is mandatory. You can start with a conversation and add material later.

Only share material you are permitted and comfortable to use with the chosen agent. Do not export an entire work mailbox, internal drive or confidential project archive. Redact employer-confidential information and other people's personal data. A short description or redacted excerpt may be enough. Tell the agent if something can inform the conversation but must not appear in a résumé or leave the workspace.

## Give your agent this orientation

Paste this block, or attach this guide and ask the agent to summarize it before starting:

> Help me with my job search as a practical career consultant. Start with what I need now, any immediate pressures and how ready I feel. Then inventory and read the career material I choose to share. Extract what it already answers before asking me to repeat it. Organize the evidence, résumé, search brief, role roster, open questions and next actions yourself. Bring me concise decisions, not an administrative project.
>
> Ask focused questions in manageable batches and explain why a detail matters. Accept “unknown,” “skip” and “later.” Some discovery may need substantial questions upfront; pace them with me and use existing material to reduce the load. Preserve useful evidence across my career, including earlier and volunteer work. Do not invent facts or imply that I personally executed all team work. Show uncertainty and ask me to confirm final claims and positioning.
>
> Explain the overall experience briefly, then tell me what we can do in this actual session, what you can help me do manually, what needs optional setup, and what you cannot verify yet. Use observed capabilities rather than brand assumptions. I can begin without more setup. Do not claim to browse, schedule, upload, share or apply unless you can do that and I authorize it. Keep all external actions separate from drafting. Use the agent-start guide in this kit for the operating process.

Add your preferences if useful: “one question at a time,” “give me a detailed explanation,” “short answers today,” “use plain language,” “screen-reader-friendly output,” or “I have twenty minutes.” You can change the pace at any point.

If you do not know where to begin, say: “Read what I have and summarize my experience for me. Help me notice strengths I may be overlooking.” You do not need to choose one target title first; the agent can explore possible roles alongside intake.

## What the experience should feel like

First, the agent asks what would help now. If you have an urgent application, it can focus on that while saving broader career work for later. If you are unsure of direction, it can help identify capabilities and possible role families before asking you to choose a title.

Next, it reads what you supplied and tells you what it already knows, what conflicts and what actually needs your answer. You should not have to retype an old résumé into a questionnaire or set up folders and trackers before getting help. The agent maintains the running notes and proposes the next useful step.

You may have a substantial conversation about your experience, goals and constraints. The aim is useful discovery, not eliminating questions. Good questions come in a sensible order, with room to pause. You can approve a small batch of facts or choose between two directions without resolving everything at once.

## A fictional first three turns

**Turn 1 — You:** “I have two older résumés and scattered notes. I don't know what title to search for. I have twenty minutes.”

**Agent:** “Share whichever material you are comfortable using; I will reconcile it and keep track. We can explore roles without picking one title today. What most needs to change in your next role?”

**Turn 2 — You:** “More coaching and developing people, less routine administration. Here are the résumés and notes.”

**Agent:** “I've read both résumés and combined the timeline, with one date left open for later. The older résumé has coaching work missing from the newer one. That gives us two families to explore: team development and operations programs. Those are directions, not current openings. In the coaching work, what outcome were you accountable for?”

**Turn 3 — You:** “I developed supervisors and helped them build a consistent coaching practice. They managed the day-to-day delivery.”

**Agent:** “I'll preserve your leadership accountability and the supervisors' delivery. I've saved that evidence and the two directions. Next I'll bring you a short experience summary to approve and a few search ideas. You do not need to organize the notes or choose a title before we continue.”

## When you come back

You can say, “Pick up where we left off. I have ten minutes.” A useful check-in is: “We have your approved summary and two role families. Has anything changed? We can review a few roles, develop one accomplishment, or finish a packet.” The agent should recover current state without making you reconstruct the previous session. When roles exist, ask for a compact roster showing status, fit, latest check and one decision per role.

You decide the facts, positioning, roles worth pursuing and material you want shared. A cover letter is optional unless required or useful for your purpose. Reminders are optional too: agree on the timing, timezone and channel before anything is scheduled. In this kit's local version, listing a next action does not schedule a notification.

## Help the process work better for you

You can ask, “What could work better here?” The agent should give a short review of one useful friction point, suggest one small change and explain how you would tell whether it helped. That might mean fewer repeated questions, clearer commute information before roles reach you, or shorter check-ins. You can keep, trial, decline or later revert a change.

Changes to your goals, filters, reminder cadence, privacy or external actions need your approval. The agent should keep personal adaptations in your private workspace and preserve the previous version. It should not quietly loosen approvals, chase application volume, or turn every session into a retrospective. One callback or rejection is tentative feedback, not a reason to permanently rerank your options or claim a change caused the outcome. Nothing is automatically sent back to the kit's authors.

When you switch chats or providers, give the next agent your private checkpoint and capability record if you are comfortable sharing them. It should reuse confirmed information and recheck its own capabilities, rather than restart the whole intake or assume the old tools are still available.

The guides and local source files are ready to review. Live research and external actions depend on the agent's capabilities and your permission. Continue with the agent at your own pace; the technical [README](README.md) is there when you want it.
